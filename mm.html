Client work, live
Research, web design and build

A site that turns a nervous learner into a booked lesson.

A driving instructor's website, redesigned as a set of British road signs so a nervous learner can find their way to a booked first lesson.

Try the live site (opens in a new tab)

  • 100Lighthouse for performance, accessibility and SEO on a phone, up from 78 and 91
  • 6towns, on one roundabout sign
  • 0backend for the client to pay for

Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, CSS Modules, Public Sans and Formspree.

Live since October 2026. Pass rates and reviews go on the site once the instructor confirms them, not before.

The brief

A site a nervous learner, or their parent, would trust.

An instructor covering six East Midlands towns needed a site that turns a visit into a booked first lesson. My first version did the job, but when I reviewed it against lists of common template patterns it matched fourteen of them, and on a phone it scored 78 for performance and 91 for accessibility.

So I started again from research: 17 driving-school sites and 11 others measured, how learners actually choose an instructor, and what stops people sending an enquiry.

The idea

Every learner already knows how to read a road sign.

The whole site is built from the British road sign system. One typeface, and colour with one job each: green is the main surface, yellow is only ever something you can press, blue is information, and red is kept for the L plate and errors.

The client asked for no photo, so trust comes from what can be checked instead: "Check me before you book" explains the green badge every approved instructor has to show.

Key decisions

Pick a lane, and the form already knows.

The three ways to pay are a motorway gantry, one lane each, with yellow used only for the recommended lane. Choosing a lane, or a kind of lesson, carries that choice down into the enquiry form, so nobody has to say it twice.

The six towns are the exits of a roundabout sign, each named, for local search and for a parent scanning for their own town.

Nothing on the page should make a nervous learner do extra work.

Answer the worries first

The whole road to passing, before they pick up the phone.

A dark road runs from provisional licence to passed in five stops. Yellow stops are the ones with the instructor; the rest link straight to GOV.UK, so a learner can see what is theirs to do.

The questions people ask most sit underneath, including one written for parents booking for their son or daughter.

Booking

A form that never loses what someone typed.

Countdown markers, like the ones before a motorway exit, say what happens after you press send. The form has every state: mistakes explained in plain words, sending, sent, failed, offline, and a draft that survives a refresh.

WhatsApp sits beside the form as an equal way in, because plenty of learners would rather message than fill in fields.

A real constraint

There was no budget for a backend, so there is no backend.

The site is fully static and the enquiry form runs through a form service. That one constraint shaped everything downstream: no accounts, no personalisation, nothing that assumes infrastructure the client isn't paying for.

Signs are put up as you scroll, painted on rather than faded in, and the whole page is checked by 83 automated tests covering the form, layout, tap sizes and every screen width before anything goes live.
